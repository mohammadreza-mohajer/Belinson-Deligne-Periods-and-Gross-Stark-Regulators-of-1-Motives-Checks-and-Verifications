# Arithmetic foundations and scope

The project imports Lean and mathlib. The arithmetic works in the following table supply inputs to the manuscript. Their statements enter the formal correspondence at the specified points. Every hypothesis of a Lean theorem can be inspected in its declaration. The companion introduces no global axioms for these inputs.

| Paper sections | Arithmetic input used by the paper | Use in the formal argument |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2, 3 | Deligne, *Théorie de Hodge III*, §10. Scholl, *Remarks on special values of L-functions*, §II, Conjecture A. Dirichlet's unit theorem and the analytic class number formula. | Identifying an inertia kernel with a toric valuation kernel supplies the diagonal Frobenius modules. A norm diagram and criticality supply the maps in `BD.Linear`. |
| 4 | Deligne and Ribet on interpolation, Cassou-Noguès on p-adic L-functions, Colmez on the p-adic residue, pp. 371–372, and Brumer on Leopoldt and logarithms. | The quotient, finite Taylor factors, and arithmetic identification of the regulator come from these results. `BD.Series` checks their formal multiplication, cancellation and coefficient extraction. |
| 4, 5 | Baker and Brumer on linear forms in logarithms, Fuchs and Pham, Theorem 1.1 and §3.1. | The rational valuation argument and the odd/even kernel calculation precede the linear independence input. |
| 5 | Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, Chapter I, §2. Mohajer, *Semistable 1-Motives*, equation (2.3) and Proposition 7.5. | The signed local Tate pairing gives the row `xᵀV + yᵀL`. The library proves its graph and the determinant identity for every finite index set. [Mohajer’s preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17685) |
| 5, 6 | Gross on p-adic L-series at zero, Spiess and Charollois–Dasgupta on the order bound, Dasgupta–Kakde–Ventullo, Theorem 1 and equation (8), and Maksoud, Theorem 4.3. | Residue-degree normalization, expected Taylor coefficient and bounds on regulator kernels. The axioms report concerns proof terms in Lean. The arithmetic inputs here have separate entries in the correspondence. [Gross–Stark theorem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2018/188-3/p03) |
| 6 | Calegari and Mazur, *Nearly ordinary Galois deformations over arbitrary number fields*, Conjecture 3.10, and Maksoud, §2, on weak p-adic Schanuel. | The conditional argument needs independence of the numerical regulator entries. The generic determinant splitting and exponent-kernel theorem are unconditional. [Calegari–Mazur](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1474748008000327) |
| 7 | Deligne–Ribet interpolation and algebraicity of the sampled special values, the Artin functional equation, Chebotarev density and odd Brauer induction. | Scalar descent and the analytic isolation and polynomial-separation arguments have proofs here. Producing the isolating data from the particular Artin functions is the outstanding arithmetic step. |
| 8 | Euler's criterion, the generalized Bernoulli formula and Washington, Chapter 4 and Theorem 7.10. | The finite character sum uses a symbol proved equal to mathlib's Legendre symbol. The convergent series and its difference quotient in the integral coordinate have direct Lean proofs. Identifying the finite expressions with complex L-values and passing to the derivative in the cyclotomic parameter uses the interpolation and logarithm theorems. |
| 9 | Modularity and rational modular symbols, Greenberg–Stevens, Theorem 0.3 for p ≥ 5, Kobayashi, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 for odd p, and the p-adic Mahler–Manin result cited in the paper. | The formal order statements use nonzero ordinary interpolation factors or a first Taylor coefficient at split multiplicative reduction. The results about the elliptic p-adic L-function itself are arithmetic inputs. |

The bibliographic details, conventions for twists, and hypotheses for each application are in the [manuscript](../paper/manuscript.pdf). The [statement correspondence](CORRESPONDENCE.md) records the scope of every numbered item and labelled formula.
