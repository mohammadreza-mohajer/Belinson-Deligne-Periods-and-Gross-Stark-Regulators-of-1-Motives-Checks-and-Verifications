# Overview of the formal arguments

The paper asks what a mixed Euler factor, a regulator at zero, and a cyclotomic function before specialization each tell us about a 1-motive. The companion proves algebraic and analytic arguments for each object and supplies exact arithmetic for the examples. The declarations are written over general fields or rings when the proof allows that generality.

## From a valuation to an order at zero

In a basis adapted to two Frobenius-stable subspaces, the Euler determinant of an upper triangular block matrix splits into the product of the two diagonal Euler determinants. Comparing the bases supplied by the inertia and valuation exact sequences gives the division-free identity `M * D = G * Y`. This identity can be specialized even when the correction factor vanishes. The arithmetic construction of the two exact sequences appears in Section 2 of the manuscript. [Euler determinants](../BD/Euler.lean)

For the critical-rank argument, take a lattice valuation map `v : U → D` followed by the split real projection `p : D → V`. Surjectivity of the composite forces the divisor dimension to be at least the split rank. At equal dimension, the projection from divisors is an isomorphism and the kernel of the composite equals the kernel of the valuation. The proof of relative lifting corrects an absolute lift by a norm section which preserves the zero-divisor subspace. [Norm diagrams and dimensions](../BD/Linear.lean)

In the Taylor ring, `HasTerm F r a` means that `F = X^r U` for a series with constant coefficient `a`. The definition permits `a = 0`. It follows that the coefficient of order `r` equals `a` and that the order is exactly `r` precisely when `a` is nonzero. Finite products, powers and cancellation of the split poles are proved for any finite index set and any admissible ranks. The applications to the actual mixed L-function require the analytic and arithmetic identifications in Sections 3 and 4. [Formal leading terms](../BD/Series.lean)

## Local lifts and principal minors

Write the signed cup-product obstruction as the row `xᵀV + yᵀL`, where the residue degrees are included in the valuation matrix `V`. If `L = BV` and `V` has a right inverse, the obstruction vanishes exactly when `x = −Bᵀy`. This proof checks the transpose and minus sign. A change of logarithm branch subtracts a scalar matrix. [Regulator coordinates](../BD/Matrices.lean)

For a finite set of indices, multilinearity in the rows expands the determinant of `diag(t) − B` over subsets. Rows taken from the diagonal produce a triangular block after reindexing by the subset and its complement. The other diagonal block is the corresponding principal submatrix of `−B`. Its determinant contributes the sign `(−1)^|J|`. The proof includes the empty subset and rank zero. The equivalence between exact analytic order and absence of a pure logarithm lift follows once the Gross–Stark Taylor coefficient has been identified. [Principal minors](../BD/Matrices.lean) · [Application to exact order](../BD/Applications.lean)

## Recovering blocks and coefficient fields

Once an odd representation has been decomposed into simultaneous blocks, its multiplicities enter the characteristic polynomial as exponents and its trace as integer weights. The determinant and characteristic-polynomial proofs allow the dimensions of the blocks to vary and include multiplicity zero. For the coefficient field, a Galois automorphism fixes the field generated by a trace precisely when it fixes the trace. If those automorphisms are exactly the ones fixing every character value, Galois correspondence identifies the two generated fields. Applying this equality to a regulator requires the equivariant arithmetic labelling in Corollary 6.7. [Repeated blocks](../BD/Blocks.lean) · [Galois field argument](../BD/CoefficientField.lean)

## A complete algebraic kernel

For an additive monoid map `w : A → B`, the induced map on monoid algebras adds coefficients in each fiber of `w`. Choose a representative for every inhabited fiber. Subtracting a monomial at its representative from the original monomial gives a fiber binomial. Summing these differences reduces any polynomial in the kernel to zero. Lean proves equality of the kernel and the ideal generated by the fiber binomials over any commutative coefficient ring. For disjoint generic blocks of positive size, specializing each block to a diagonal matrix gives a separate polynomial left inverse for the map sending variables to their determinants. Evaluation of the variables at monomials is injective precisely when the map of exponent monoids is injective. With mathlib’s definition of algebraic independence, this identifies which monomial families are algebraically independent over any nontrivial commutative coefficient ring. Theorems 6.8 and 7.1 use these algebraic statements. [Exponent kernels](../BD/Relations.lean) · [Generic determinants](../BD/GenericDet.lean) · [Independence criterion](../BD/Monomials.lean)

## Isolating the functional relations

A nonzero null vector for a system of algebraic rows over an extension field has a nonzero coordinate in some basis over the base field. Applying the corresponding coordinate functional to every entry produces a nonzero null vector over the base. This proof works for infinitely many rows. For an escaping sequence, a polynomial tending to zero vanishes. The proof repeatedly removes the constant term and divides by the variable. Absolute summability bounds a family of smaller frequencies by a geometric envelope with an arbitrary fixed polynomial prefactor. [Descent and escaping polynomials](../BD/Interpolation.lean) · [Bounds for tails](../BD/Tails.lean)

After isolating one frequency, arbitrarily many products of character coordinates separate finitely many distinct tuples. For each tuple the proof constructs a polynomial taking value one there and zero on every other tuple. The theorem `isolateScale` combines this separation with the vanishing-tail lemma to eliminate all polynomial coefficients at a leading scale. Its hypotheses spell out the normalized samples needed for every character monomial. Deriving those samples for Deligne–Ribet germs uses interpolation, the Artin functional equation and Chebotarev in the manuscript. [Character separation](../BD/Separation.lean) · [Isolation theorem](../BD/Artin.lean)

## The first derivative at seven

The quadratic character of discriminant −687 has residues given by the product of the Legendre symbols at 3 and 229. The symbol used by the executable is identified with mathlib's Legendre symbol. Lean proves that the first character power sum is −8244, the finite formula for the value at zero gives 12, and the formula at −6 gives −10344805682225424. Lean evaluates mathlib’s first and seventh Bernoulli polynomials directly in the finite character sum. The independent Python calculation uses the Kronecker symbol and exponential generating series for all three negative interpolation indices. [Legendre bridge](../BD/Legendre.lean) · [Bernoulli-polynomial sums](../BD/SpecialValues.lean) · [Exact arithmetic](../BD/Arithmetic.lean) · [Independent calculation](../scripts/arithmetic.py)

In the ring of integers modulo 49 the square of `7s` is zero. Evaluation of an arbitrary formal power series on this square-zero element is a ring homomorphism and depends only on the first two coefficients. With constant 24 and value 3 at the input corresponding to −6, the unnormalized slope is 28 modulo 49. Dividing by 24 gives 42 modulo 49. A product identity with an extra factor of constant one therefore has distinct linear coefficients when the two relevant factors are nonzero. The p-adic interpolation theorem identifies this formal jet with the arithmetic derivative. [Square-zero evaluation](../BD/Jet.lean) · [Exact reference outputs](../results/lean-calculations.txt)


The same calculation holds for convergent power series in the actual p-adic integers. Write the series as its constant and linear terms plus the square of the argument times a second convergent series. The second series has integral values, which bounds the error in the difference quotient and proves that the limit is the linear coefficient. Reduction to integers modulo the square of the prime kills the entire quadratic remainder. For p = 7 the equation characterizing the interpolation coordinate gives the input needed to recover the slope. The change to the cyclotomic variable still uses the logarithm of 8. [Convergence, differentiation and reduction](../BD/PadicJet.lean)
